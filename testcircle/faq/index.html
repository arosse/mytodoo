Getting started

What is TestCircle?

TestCircle is an app that brings indie Android developers together to get through Google Play's closed testing requirement (12 testers for 14 days). You join a testing group of up to 16 developers, everyone installs and tests each other's apps over 14 days, and everyone shares feedback along the way.

Does TestCircle work?

Yes, and we have the numbers to show it. As of October 2026, 80 testing group cycles have been completed, and over 900 apps that went through a TestCircle testing group are confirmed public on Google Play (in production). Members have made over 19,000 app installs and 70,000 app tests through TestCircle. See the live numbers on the TestCircle page.

How is TestCircle different from Testers Community, App Hive and other "12 Testers" apps?

TestCircle is a group testing app with a flexible 14-day cycle: instead of testing every day, testers work through five stages, which leaves time for real use and better feedback.

Most closed testing services work in one of four ways:

TypeExamplesHow it worksThe catch
Credit systemsMany "12 Testers" apps, and Single Tests in TestCircleTest other apps to earn credits, spend credits to get installsInstalls are guaranteed, but regular use isn't. Google can ask for more testing because of "insufficient tester engagement".

Many testers end up opening each app, taking a screenshot and closing it, without really testing or giving useful feedback.
One-to-one test swapsSome "12 Testers" appsThe app pairs developers one by one, and each pair tests each other's apps every day for 14 days. Daily testing is tracked.Swaps start at different times, so nothing guarantees 12 testers opted in over the same 14 days, which is what Google requires.
Paid tester servicesPaid plans in some "12 Testers" apps, freelance marketplacesYou pay a fee, and the service supplies 12 or more testers for 14 daysIt costs money, and you can't check how well your app is really tested: the testers have no stake in your app, so real use and useful feedback aren't guaranteed.

TestCircle's group testing works differently:

  • Flexible: each stage gives you 48 or 72 hours, and you test at times that suit you.
  • Five stages instead of daily testing: three feedback stages and two Quick Tests (3, 2, 3, 2 and 3 days).
  • Better feedback, enough use: less frequent testing leaves time for real use and constructive feedback, while keeping enough regular use for production access. In the large majority of cases, members who stay active get it.
  • Everyone starts together: as in other group systems, and unlike one-to-one swaps, a group of up to 16 developers starts on the same day, so all testers are opted in over the same 14 days.
  • Checked testing: installs are checked on each tester's phone, and testing with usage time or screenshots.

TestCircle also offers Single Tests, a credit system, to add extra installs and feedback on top of a group.

How is TestCircle different from finding testers on Reddit or forums?

Finding testers on Reddit or developer forums costs nothing, but it has three problems:

  • No check that apps are really tested. Many testers install the app, take a screenshot of the welcome screen and go no further. Constructive feedback is rare.
  • No fair test-for-test. Nothing makes others test your app in return: you can test dozens of apps and only get a few tests back.
  • No common timing. Google requires 12 testers opted in continuously over the same 14 days. When swaps happen one by one, at different times, reaching 12 at once is hard.

In TestCircle:

  • Installs are checked on each tester's phone, testing is checked with usage time or screenshots, and feedback is written in guided forms.
  • Members who stop testing are removed from the group, and you can skip testing the apps of members who didn't install or test yours.
  • Your whole group starts on the same day, so all testers are opted in over the same 14 days.

Is daily testing necessary to meet Google Play's requirements?

No. Google asks for at least 12 testers who stay opted in continuously for at least 14 days, and it looks at how engaged they were: with too little real use, it can ask for more testing. So regular testing across the 14 days matters, but testing every day isn't required. That's why TestCircle spreads testing over five stages instead of asking you to test every day.

How do I get started?
  1. Install TestCircle from Google Play and sign in with your Google account.
  2. Add testcircle-testers@googlegroups.com as a tester on your app's closed testing track in Play Console.
  3. Join the Google Group https://groups.google.com/g/testcircle-testers with the account signed in to your phone's Play Store.
  4. In TestCircle, press Join on the Group Testing tab and submit your app.

The app guides you through each step.

Do I need to send my app link, opt-in link or a screenshot to anyone?

No. TestCircle doesn't work through one-to-one swaps. All testing happens inside the app, with the members of your testing group. You only give your app's package name when you submit it, and TestCircle creates the links for everyone.

There's no TestCircle opt-in link to join either: TestCircle itself isn't looking for testers. It's the place where you meet other developers who are.

What do I enter in the submission form?

Your app's name, its package name (for example com.yourname.yourapp, not a link) and its icon. If your app needs a login, add a test account in the test credentials field. You can add instructions for testers in the additional info field.

Which Google account should I use?

You can sign in to TestCircle with any Google account. The account that matters is the one signed in to your phone's Play Store: join the Google Group with that account, or the other apps won't be available to you. After joining, it can take a few hours before the apps become available.

If you joined with the wrong account, just join the Google Group again with your Play Store account. Nothing needs to change in TestCircle. Your Play Console account doesn't need to be the same account either.

I joined the Google Group but I don't see anything in it. Is that normal?

Yes. The Google Group only gives access to the apps in closed testing. There's nothing to read or post there: everything happens in the TestCircle app.

I already have my own testers. Can I still use TestCircle?

Yes, you keep your closed testing track. In Play Console, a track's testers come either from email lists or from Google Groups, not both:

  • If you already use a Google Group, add testcircle-testers@googlegroups.com next to it. Your existing testers stay.
  • If you use an email list, switch the track to Google Groups and add testcircle-testers@googlegroups.com. To keep your current testers, put them in a Google Group of your own and add that group too.

Why does "Verify Play Store Link" show "Item not found"?

The "Verify Play Store Link" button opens your app's Google Play page from the package name you entered.

If the package name is wrong, the page will never open. Copy it exactly from Play Console (for example com.yourname.yourapp).

If the package name is right, the page opens once your TestCircle setup is complete:

  1. testcircle-testers@googlegroups.com is added as a tester on your app's closed testing track in Play Console, and the release on that track is rolled out.
  2. You've joined the Google Group https://groups.google.com/g/testcircle-testers with the account signed in to your phone's Play Store.

If you've just made these changes, wait a few hours and try again: Google Play takes time to update.

If your phone's account is already a tester through your own email list or Google Group, the page can open even before the TestCircle setup is done. Make sure both steps above are done anyway, otherwise the members of your group won't be able to install your app.

If your app is already public (in production), the page always opens, so the check only confirms the package name.

I get an error when I try to join a group.

The group probably filled up while you were joining. Go back, or restart the app, and join the group that's open now.

Does my app need to meet any requirements?
  • It must be on Google Play, in closed testing or already public (in production), and available in all countries.
  • No illegal or harmful content.
  • If it needs a login, the test credentials you provide must work.

Any kind of app works, including web apps packaged for Google Play (TWA): TestCircle only uses the package name. Any language is fine; adding a short English testing guide helps testers. For paid apps, set up licence testing so testers can use a test card and pay nothing.

Can I choose which countries my testers come from?

No. Groups aren't matched by country, to keep as many developers as possible in each group. That's why apps must be available in all countries.

My app is already public (in production). Can I still use TestCircle?

Yes. You can join a testing group and go through a 14-day cycle anyway: you get installs, regular use for two weeks and feedback from other developers. You don't need to add the Google Group in Play Console, but you still need to join it to access the other members' apps. You can also use Single Tests (see below).

Is TestCircle available on iOS?

No. TestCircle is built for Google Play closed testing, so it's Android only.

Can I use TestCircle on an emulator?

TestCircle should be used on a physical Android phone. On emulators, some features (such as submitting your app) may not work.

The testing cycle

How does a testing cycle work?
  1. Group forming: you join the open group. As other members join, install their apps as they appear.
  2. Install stage (24 hours): once the group starts, everyone finishes installing every other member's app. TestCircle checks each install on the tester's phone.
  3. 14-day cycle, five stages: First Impressions (3 days), Quick Test (2 days), Feature Deep-Dive (3 days), Quick Test (2 days), Final Review (3 days). The app tells you what to do and notifies you when something is due.
  4. Results: you get an AI-generated summary of the feedback on your app, and you can export a report as PDF or CSV.

You can message the other developers in your group in the app at any time.

How long until my group starts?

A group starts as soon as it has 16 members, or when its joining window closes with enough active members (minimum 14). As of October 2026, one or two new groups start every day, so the wait is usually less than 24 hours.

How many testers will I get?

Groups aim for 16 developers, so up to 15 others install and test your app. That leaves a margin above the 12 that Google asks for, in case a few people drop out during the two weeks.

What if testers drop out during the 14 days?

Nobody is added to a group after it starts, so dropouts aren't replaced. That's why groups aim for 16 members: a margin above Google's 12. Members who stop testing are removed, and everyone has a reason to stay active, since their own app needs the testers too. A few extra testers of your own (friends, family, your own devices) or a Single Test give you an extra safety margin.

How many apps will I test, and how much time does it take?

You test every other app in your group, up to 15. Testing is flexible: unlike services that ask you to test every day, TestCircle gives you 48 or 72 hours for each stage, and you choose when to do the tasks within that window: all in one sitting, or a few apps at a time, whatever fits your schedule.

There are two kinds of tasks:

  • Feedback stages (3 stages, 72 hours each): use each app for a short time and write a few lines of feedback. A few minutes per app, so about 30 to 45 minutes per stage for a full group.
  • Quick Tests (2 stages, 48 hours each): just open each app for a few seconds. Takes less than 5 minutes per stage.

Including installing the apps at the start, that's around 2 to 3 hours of testing in total, spread over two weeks at times that suit you.

What happens if I miss deadlines or stop testing?

Members who stop testing, or who haven't installed the other apps when the group starts, are removed from the group. Once you're removed, the other members stop testing your app. Testing works both ways: if someone didn't install or test your app, you can skip testing theirs.

What if I can't install an app in my group?

Report it in the app (for example, a broken link or "not available in your country"). You'll be asked to try again later. If it still fails, choose "I still cannot install": the task counts as done, with no penalty and no testing needed.

If an app in your group is paid, choose the test card at checkout, or follow the instructions the developer gives (for example a promo code). Never pay with your own money to test an app.

What if other members can't install my app?

Check your setup: the Google Group added as a tester on your closed testing track, all countries enabled, and the release rolled out. If none of the other group members can install your app before testing starts, your app may be automatically removed from the group. In this case, you can take the time to fix your app's setup and join the next group.

The app says some members haven't installed my app yet. What should I do?

Check your Play Console dashboard. If it says "12 testers have opted in", you're fine. Installs from outside TestCircle (your own devices, friends) count too. Members who don't install the other apps are removed from the group. For an extra margin, you can also post your app in Single Tests.

Why does Play Console only show "12 testers have opted in"?

That counter never goes above 12, even with 100 or more testers. For your real numbers, look at the statistics section of Play Console (but these are often delayed by a few days and not always very accurate or reliable). TestCircle can't see Google's opt-in data, so make sure to keep an eye on your Play Console dashboard.

How is testing checked?

With the time each app spends on screen, if the tester allows usage access, or with a screenshot. Installs are checked on each tester's phone.

Does TestCircle guarantee production access?

No. Only Google decides. TestCircle gives you what Google asks for (enough testers, using your app regularly over 14 days) and real feedback to show for it.

What happens when my cycle ends?

On the Cycle Complete screen, press "Go to Lobby" to join a new group with your next app. Your finished app stays in the My App tab. When Google approves production access, press "I got production access" there to have your app featured in TestCircle.

Credits and cost

Is TestCircle free?

Yes. You don't need to pay anything to get your app through closed testing: group testing is free, and in the large majority of cases it's all you need to meet Google Play's requirements.

There are no ads, no subscription, no sign-up fee and no payment details. New accounts get 50 credits, which cover joining your first group, so you can complete a full cycle without paying. Joining later groups costs 50 credits, which you earn for free by testing other apps.

Purchases (credit packs, visibility boosts, tips) are optional extras: you don't need them to pass closed testing. They go through Google Play's checkout and are never automatic.

What are credits, and how do I earn them?

Credits are virtual and have no cash value. You earn them by testing apps in Single Tests (15 credits or more per test, a few minutes each), or you can buy a pack in the app if you prefer to skip the testing.

What costs credits:

  • Joining a testing group: 50 (only taken if the join succeeds)
  • Detailed feedback for stage 2 and stage 3: 30 each (stage 1 feedback and the AI-generated test feedback summary are free)
  • Posting your app in Single Tests: 150

Why do some features cost credits?

Running TestCircle has real server costs. To keep TestCircle free of ads, those costs are covered by optional purchases: credit packs and Single Test visibility boosts.

Because TestCircle is a developer community, everything that costs credits can also be unlocked without paying: you earn credits by testing other developers' apps. Each test you do helps another developer through closed testing, so credits work as community solidarity: the more you help others, the more you can use.

The alternative would have been to show ads. We chose to keep TestCircle ad-free.

What are Single Tests?

A list of apps that anyone can test once: install, open, test for at least a minute and leave feedback. Testing apps in Single Tests earns you credits. You can also post your own app, in closed testing or already public (in production), to get extra installs and feedback from more users. Single Tests don't guarantee retention or regular use over 14 days, so they complement a testing group but don't replace one.

If your app is only available in some countries, say so in the instructions. Testers elsewhere will report that they can't install it, so you may get fewer tests, since fewer testers can reach your app.

Can I test more than one app at a time?

You can be in only one testing group at a time, since each group already means testing up to 15 apps over two weeks. You can post other apps in Single Tests at any time, and join a new group with your next app when your cycle ends.

I submitted the wrong app. Can I change it?

Apps can't be swapped after you join a group. You may however contact Support at greenantapps@gmail.com, and we'll try to help you sort it out if you made a mistake.

Privacy and contact

Can I save my testing feedback?

Yes, and it's a good idea to do it. In the My App tab, tap CSV or PDF under your app to export a report, then choose Share or Save to Device. This works for group testing (from the first feedback stage onward) and for each of your apps in Single Tests. The report includes all the feedback you've unlocked, and the AI-generated summary for group testing.

Save your report in time: testing data is deleted automatically, as explained in the privacy policy:

  • Group testing feedback: about 30 days after your testing cycle ends.
  • Single Tests: listings and their reports, 30 days after submission (resubmitting or boosting a listing restarts the 30 days).
  • Screenshots: 30 days after they're uploaded.

AI-generated summaries are kept for as long as your account exists.

What data does TestCircle collect?

You sign in with Google. We store your email address, name and profile photo for login and account management only. Other developers never see them: in testing groups they only see your app's name and icon. Feedback text (with no names) is processed by Google's Gemini AI to create summaries and translations. Screenshots and chat messages are deleted after 30 days. We don't sell data or use it for advertising. Full details: Privacy policy.
